# Candidate decipherment protocol

A candidate solution is an explicitly bounded hypothesis. Supply the full reading/segmentation procedure, key or language assumptions, allowed exceptions, training material, all attempts within the declared search scope, and the evidence that would refute it. Do not treat selected plausible words, illustrations, names, anagrams or an unconstrained language-model narrative as validation.

Before inspecting the held-out target, freeze a passage/folio partition, prediction format, permitted uncertainty and evaluation rule. Recover authored known plaintext through the same instrument. Include decoy languages or texts and equal search budgets when relevant. Separate material dating, historical plausibility and image identification from textual predictions.

Publish successful, failed and ambiguous predictions; quantify discretionary choices and error rates without pretending a ground-truth translation exists. Later revisions become new disclosed hypotheses and do not erase prior failures. An independent implementation, manuscript-domain expertise and external scholarly review are required before a project-wide major-advance or translation claim. AI role-play does not supply those forms of independence.

The initial project claims no accepted decipherment and no percentage translated. Publication credit follows the separate contribution and authorship policies.
